======================================================================
MassCascadeMC -- NUMERICAL METHODS, IMPLEMENTATION CHOICES, SAFEGUARDS
======================================================================

The notes below document the numerical construction of the Monte Carlo
engine in masscascade.py, the modelling choices that enter the cascade,
and the safeguards used to distinguish physical behaviour from numerical
artefacts.

The note numbers are referenced from comments in the source.  They are
stable: new notes are appended, existing numbers are not reused.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[1] MAJORANT COLLISION FREQUENCY (MCF) / MAJORANT-KERNEL SAMPLING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Instead of computing and sampling the exact interaction rate of every
particle pair directly, an upper-bound kernel

                    fmaj >= K

is used to generate trial pairs.  A selected pair (i,j) is accepted with
probability

                    P_accept = K_ij / fmaj_ij .

The majorant determines the efficiency of the sampling, but not the
physical interaction rate.  Provided that fmaj is a valid upper bound,
the majorant cancels between pair selection and acceptance.

The construction follows the Majorant Collision Frequency idea used in
DSMC and stochastic coagulation methods:

  - Ivanov & Rogasinsky, Sov. J. Numer. Anal. Math. Modelling 3, 453
    (1988)                                      [MCF scheme in DSMC]
  - Garcia, van den Broeck, Aertsens & Serneels, Physica A 143, 535
    (1987)                                      [majorant for coagulation]
  - Gillespie, J. Atmos. Sci. 32, 1977 (1975)
                                            [exact stochastic coalescence]

The implementation uses the same majorant-rate construction for pair
selection while advancing the clock by the mean trial waiting time
rather than drawing an additional exponential deviate; see note [5].


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[2] BIN-PAIR MAJORANT TABLE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The majorant is not a single global number.  It is a matrix

                    fmaj[b,c]

over pairs of logarithmic mass bins.  Each entry is constructed from the
upper mass corners of the two bins and bounds the kernel for every
particle pair inside that bin pair.

This has two purposes.

  1. EFFICIENCY.  A global f_max = K(m_max, m_max) is formally valid for
     a monotone kernel but becomes very loose over a broad mass range.
     For a homogeneous kernel a typical acceptance scales approximately
     as

                    <K>/f_max ~ (m0/m_max)^lambda,

     which falls by a factor 10^(-lambda) per decade covered by the
     calculation.  With the bin table the corner overshoot is only
     10^(0.1 lambda) per argument on a 0.1 dex grid.

  2. A CHECKABLE BOUND.  Because the table is fixed by the bin geometry,
     every trial can verify explicitly that

                    K_ij <= fmaj_ij.

     A violation raises an error rather than silently introducing a
     biased acceptance probability.  A running global maximum would not
     have this property: if it is not refreshed before a particle grows
     past it, fmaj < K and the thinning is wrong without any symptom.

The majorant cancels exactly from the physical pair rate:

    (w R / 2V) * (fmaj_ij / R) * (K_ij / fmaj_ij)  =  w K_ij / (2V).

The three factors are the total trial rate, the probability of selecting
that pair, and its acceptance probability.  Any valid majorant therefore
gives the same physical dynamics; a poor majorant only increases the
number of rejected trials.  This cancellation requires the clock to
advance on every TRIAL, including rejected trials; see note [5iv].

One further consequence of a loose majorant is that the stall guard
misfires: max_stall_tries counts trials without an accepted event, so at
a very low acceptance a healthy run stops with the diagnosis
"acceptance collapsed".

For a binned efficient implementation of this construction, see

  - Eibeck & Wagner, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 22, 802 (2000),
    doi:10.1137/S1064827599353488


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[3] O(1) BIN MEMBERSHIP WITH SWAP-WITH-LAST
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each logarithmic mass bin stores a list of particle indices, `bins[b]`,
and every particle stores its current position inside that list in
`pos_in_bin`.

Removal uses swap-with-last ("swap-and-pop"): the last particle in the
bin list is moved into the position of the removed particle and the list
is shortened by one element.

Addition and removal are therefore O(1), with no search through the
particles in the bin and no reallocation of the population arrays.  This
matters because essentially every accepted coagulation or fragmentation
event changes one or more bin memberships.

The data-structure idea is standard; for a related use together with
tree-based collision selection see

  - Goodson & Kraft, J. Comput. Phys. 183, 210 (2002).


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[4] INCREMENTAL RATE MAINTENANCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Define

                    T[b] = sum_c fmaj[b,c] N_c ,

where N_c is the occupation of mass bin c.

T is not recomputed from the full matrix after every event.  When the
population of one bin changes, the entire vector T is updated by a single
vector operation using the corresponding column of the majorant table.

The pair-selection weights are then constructed from T and sampled with
vectorised cumulative sums and `searchsorted`, rather than with a Python
loop over mass bins.

The bookkeeping cost therefore scales with the number of bins B, rather
than with the number of particle pairs.

For substantially larger B, the same cumulative-rate problem can be
handled with a Fenwick/binary tree and O(log B) updates; see

  - Goodson & Kraft, J. Comput. Phys. 183, 210 (2002).


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[5] THE PHYSICAL CLOCK
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The clock is the part of a majorant calculation in which the
pair-selection distribution and the physical interaction rate must
remain consistent.

  (i) ORDERED VS UNORDERED PAIRS.

      The maintained quantity

          R = sum_b N_b (T_b - fmaj[b,b]) = sum_{i != j} fmaj_ij

      counts ordered pairs, so every physical unordered pair appears
      twice.  The corresponding mean trial-time increment is

                    dt = 2 V / (w R).

      The factor of two affects only the physical time scale; pair
      selection itself is unchanged because both orderings represent the
      same physical pair.  Omitting it rescales t by exactly 2 and breaks
      any comparison with an analytic solution.

  (ii) PARTICLE WEIGHT.

      The implementation keeps a constant particle weight w.  For the
      baseline calculations w = 1, so one simulated particle represents
      one physical particle.  Nothing in the engine ever changes w.

      Keeping w explicitly in

                    dt = 2 V / (w R)

      makes the density scaling transparent and keeps the expression
      consistent with a constant-weight formulation.

      The injection and distributed-sink channels contain w in different
      intermediate places, for a physical reason.  Injection is specified
      externally in physical particles per unit time and therefore
      converts that physical rate into a number of simulated particles.
      The distributed sink is generated from the simulated population
      itself: its physical rate contains a factor w, and conversion back
      to simulated particles removes the same factor, so the resulting
      siphon quota is independent of w.

  (iii) BINARY SCALING.

      Because collisions are binary,

                    R ~ N^2   and therefore   dt ~ N^-2.

      A clock proportional to N^-1 would correspond to a unary process
      and would produce the wrong physical evolution.

  (iv) TIME ADVANCES ON EVERY TRIAL.

      The clock advances immediately after the majorant trial rate is
      formed, before the K/fmaj acceptance test.  Rejected majorant
      trials are null events of the sampling process and consume physical
      time as well.  This is what makes the cancellation in note [2]
      work:

          trial rate
          x probability of selecting (i,j)
          x probability of accepting (i,j)    ->   w K_ij / (2V).

      Advancing the clock only for accepted events would make the
      physical time depend on the efficiency of the majorant.  The
      reported rate would be R instead of R * <K/fmaj> = sum K, and with
      a global f_max the effective step would be

                    dt_eff ~ 1 / (N^2 m_max^lambda),

      i.e. the kernel entering through the extreme of the distribution
      instead of through the typical mass.  In an open system m_max is
      pinned at the sink and does not grow, so the m0 dependence leaves
      the clock entirely, the drift degenerates to

                    dm0/dt ~ m0   (beta = 1),

      and the constant-flux argument then returns alpha = -2 for every
      kernel.  This is a purely numerical route to the same -2
      fingerprint that note [8] produces physically.

  (v) DETERMINISTIC MEAN-RATE CLOCK.

      The code advances each majorant trial by its mean waiting time,

                    dt = 2 V / (w R),

      rather than multiplying this expression by an independent
      exponential deviate.  The calculation therefore reproduces the
      mean-field collision-rate evolution while suppressing an additional
      source of clock noise.  Pair selection and event acceptance remain
      stochastic.

      This is not an exact Gillespie continuous-time Markov-chain
      realisation, in which the waiting time itself would also be
      exponentially distributed.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[6] DETERMINISTIC RESIDUAL SOURCE AND SINK
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Boundary channels whose total rates are known continuously use fractional
residual accumulators rather than independent Poisson draws.

If a channel should produce a mean number q*dt of simulated events during
one time step, its residual is updated as

                    r <- r + q dt.

The integer part fires immediately and the fractional part is carried
into the next step.  The integrated number of source/sink events
therefore follows the imposed mean rate without an additional shot-noise
component.

The same construction is used by the deterministic injector and by the
distributed first-order sink of note [16].

Removing the channel's own Poisson noise matters most for the
distributed sink, because that sink acts inside the inertial range, where
removal shot noise would contribute directly to the measured spectral
fluctuations.

This changes HOW MANY boundary events occur over a given interval, not
the stochastic rule selecting WHICH particles are involved.  The
deterministic character of the imposed injection is a modelling choice
and is recorded in the run metadata.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[7] AGE AND GENERATION BOOKKEEPING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The code carries two complementary descriptions of motion through mass
space.  Age labels an ENSEMBLE at a given time since an assigned birth or
injection clock.  Generation labels the number of mass-changing steps
separating a particle from the body that entered the system.  These
observables answer different questions and are not interchangeable
trajectory measurements.

---- AGES ------------------------------------------------------------

Every particle carries `inj_time`.  The age used by the isochrone
histogram is

                    tau = t_phys - inj_time.

In an open system, accumulation may be delayed until a prescribed
sink-throughput gate `iso_start_sink` has been reached.  A sink-count
gate states directly that material has crossed the cascade and does not
depend on the absolute clock normalisation.  A closed system has no sink,
so the default gate is disarmed there.

COAGULATION.  A merger product has two ancestors, so an explicit
inheritance rule is unavoidable.  `age_rule` may use the minimum age,
maximum age, mass-weighted age, or the age of the heavier parent.

Injection clocks are always created at m_inj.  An age class therefore
starts from a well-defined physical mass and <m>(tau) can be interpreted
as an ensemble growth law.  Because different inheritance rules weight
different parts of the merger tree, the chosen rule is reported in the
metadata and its sensitivity should be checked when an age-based growth
exponent is quoted.

FRAGMENTATION.  A fragment has one parent, but two children are produced,
so one must still decide which child retains the parent's age clock:

    'inherit'   both fragments retain the parent's clock;
    'heavier'   the heavier fragment retains it, the lighter receives a
                new clock;
    'lighter'   the lighter fragment retains it;
    'both_new'  both fragments receive new clocks.

Under 'inherit', an age class represents the whole surviving descendant
family of an injected body.  It is an ensemble over a branching tree, not
a single fragmentation trajectory, and a diagnostic of this is that the
median age equals the maximum age.  A broad mass distribution inside one
age class is therefore not by itself evidence against a multiplicative
cascade.

---- GENERATIONS -----------------------------------------------------

Every particle also carries `gen`, the number of mass-changing
generations separating it from the body that entered the system, and
`gen_time`, the time at which that ancestor entered.  A snapshot
accumulates a two-dimensional histogram in

                    (generation, mass)

in the same way that the age machinery accumulates (age, mass).

The generation variable removes the need to infer the number of
multiplicative steps from a small population of tagged tracers.  For
fragmentation,

                    x = ln(m/m0)

is a sum of logarithmic split increments; if successive increments are
independent, both <x> and Var(x) are linear in generation.

Generation is a property of every live particle, so the full population
contributes.  This avoids three limitations of sparse trajectory tracers:

  - statistics are not limited by the small number of complete trees
    surviving across the whole cascade;
  - long waiting times near the injection scale do not dominate the
    sampling merely because a tracer spends most of its time there;
  - a particle contributes while it is alive and does not need to survive
    all the way to the sink.

Accumulating age together with generation also gives <tau>(g), the mean
time required to reach generation g.  For fragmentation, schematically,

    dg/dtau ~ nu(m) ~ m^(beta-1),

while the logarithmic mass changes by an O(1) amount at every split.  For
the mass-weighted path associated with a uniform split, <ln xi> = -1/2,
giving

                    tau(g) = T [1 - exp(-g/(2b))]

for the corresponding exponential-generation model, with b the growth
exponent of note [14].

A generation histogram remains an ENSEMBLE at fixed g.  It does not
preserve the identity of a complete trajectory and therefore cannot
measure correlations between successive increments of one tagged path.
Independence is instead tested through the predicted linearity of the
generation moments.

NUMBER- AND MASS-WEIGHTING ARE DIFFERENT.  A path that selects a fragment
with probability proportional to its mass is size-biased.  For a uniform
split this gives

                    <ln xi> = -1/2,   Var(ln xi) = 1/4.

A generation histogram that counts BOTH fragments equally is
number-weighted instead and gives

                    <ln xi> = -1,     Var(ln xi) = 1.

Weighting the generation histogram by mass recovers the
trajectory-weighted values.

For an unbounded logarithmic split increment the one-step distribution is
asymmetric, with an exponential logarithmic tail, and the skewness of one
step is -2.  A rule that follows only the heavier piece is bounded,
xi in [1/2,1], and is nearly symmetric.  The skewness after g independent
steps decreases as -2/sqrt(g), so the asymmetry is a finite-generation
effect rather than a failure of the central-limit picture.

INHERITANCE.  In fragmentation both children receive gen+1 and retain
`gen_time`, so t - gen_time always measures the time since their common
ancestor entered, independently of the separate `frag_age_rule`.  In
coagulation the merger product receives

                    max(gen_i, gen_j) + 1

together with the older ancestral `gen_time`.  Newly injected particles
begin at gen = 0.  Generations above `gen_max` are collected in an
overflow counter, reported as `gen_overflow`.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[8] LOCALITY OF THE INTERACTION RULE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A scale-free stationary cascade requires the collision integral carrying
the flux to be local: interactions at mass m must not be dominated by
particles arbitrarily far away in mass.

For fragmentation, consider a rule in which the heavier member of an
interacting pair breaks irrespective of how small its collision partner
is.  The disruption rate of a particle of mass m0 contains

    nu(m0) ~ integral_{m' < m0} F(m') K(m0,m') dm'  ~  integral x^alpha dx,

with x = m'/m0 near x -> 0.  For alpha < -1 this integral diverges at the
lower limit.  The disruption rate is then controlled by the smallest
particles in the box, i.e. by the numerical sink scale rather than by m0.

The consequences are physical rather than merely numerical:

  - the local mean-field closure is no longer applicable;
  - the drift becomes proportional to m0,  dm0/dtau ~ -C m0;
  - beta -> 1 and the measured spectrum approaches alpha = -2
    independently of the intended kernel.

Such an apparent "universal" -2 spectrum is a boundary-dominated
non-local solution, not the local constant-flux solution.

The physical remedy is analogous to the catastrophic-disruption threshold
used by Dohnanyi (1969): a sufficiently small projectile cannot
catastrophically disrupt an arbitrarily larger target.  The code imposes
the scale-free condition

                    m_small >= f * m_large,

with f of order 0.1-1.  Because f is a dimensionless mass ratio, this
introduces no preferred mass scale.  The collision integral is restricted
to x in [f,1], restoring locality while preserving the homogeneity degree
of the kernel.  The expected local open-system spectrum is then

                    alpha = -(3 + lambda)/2.

The implementation of the condition, for both processes, is note [17].

In a narrow closed self-similar packet the interaction partners are
already of comparable scale, so the same non-local divergence is absent
even at f = 0.

Locality and age inheritance are separate.  The locality parameter f
changes which physical interactions are allowed and can therefore change
the spectrum.  `frag_age_rule` changes only the label carried by the
fragments and cannot change the instantaneous mass dynamics.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[9] NO POPULATION RESAMPLING OR WEIGHT THINNING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The engine avoids population-control resampling.  Every simulated
particle represents one physical particle and

                    w = 1

remains fixed throughout the calculation.

Here "no thinning" refers to population/weight thinning; it does NOT
refer to the K/fmaj rejection step of the majorant sampler in notes
[1]-[2].

The consequences are:

  + mass bookkeeping is direct: Sum(w) and Sum(w*m) are conserved to
    machine precision, `mass_drift` is identically zero, and a nonzero
    value is a bug rather than a statistical excursion;
  + no cloning, down-sampling, or adaptive particle weights can modify
    the measured spectrum;
  + the physical clock reduces to dt = 2V/R.

The cost is dynamic range.

COAGULATION.  In a closed mass-conserving population the number of live
particles decreases approximately as

                    N ~ N_i m_i / m0,

so keeping of order 100 particles alive requires m0 <= N_i/100: about
four decades at N_i = 1e6 and six at N_i = 1e8.  A run pushed past that
ends with a handful of particles and a much shorter usable plateau.

FRAGMENTATION.  The difficulty is reversed.  The number of fragments
grows roughly as the characteristic mass decreases, so memory usage
becomes the limiting resource before the desired low-mass range is
reached.

This baseline therefore trades achievable dynamic range for the simplest
statistical interpretation: no population resampling and no variable
particle weights.  A weighted or population-controlled implementation can
extend the accessible range, but then the resampling algorithm becomes
part of the numerical method and must be validated separately.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[10] SNAPSHOT PLACEMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Snapshot placement matters differently in open and closed systems.

OPEN SYSTEM.  Once a stationary state has been reached, the observable is
the instantaneous stationary spectrum averaged over post-gate snapshots.
The time increment dt does not enter the estimator, so placement is free
and snapshot_mode='events' is adequate.

CLOSED SYSTEM.  No stationary spectrum exists.  The relevant quantity is
the time-integrated superposition

                    F(m) = integral n(m,t) dt,

evaluated numerically as F(m) ~= sum_k n(m,t_k) Delta t_k.

Snapshot sampling therefore has two separate roles, which must not be
confused:

  PLACEMENT determines which stages of the evolving distribution are
  resolved.  For a given m the integrand peaks while m0 ~ m, so every
  decade of m needs snapshots at the matching decade of m0.

  WEIGHT determines the value of the integral and must always be the
  actual physical Delta t between samples.

Three placement choices are available:

  'events'   Uniform sampling in event number.  The number of events per
             unit m0 falls as 1/m0^2, so the physical time represented by
             one event grows strongly during the evolution and late
             snapshots carry most of the total Delta t weight.  The
             integral then reduces to a few self-similar packets, and the
             plateau finder can still report a narrow, internally
             consistent plateau at the wrong index.

  't_phys'   Uniform physical-time sampling.  Adequate over a narrow
             dynamic range.  Over many decades it places too few
             snapshots during the early low-mass stages: since
             t ~ m0^(1-lambda), uniform t is nearly uniform m0, and F(m)
             at small m is then assembled from the flat cores of much
             larger packets, n ~ m0^-2 Phi(m/m0 -> 0), which is
             independent of m and drives alpha towards 0.

  'log_m0'   Sampling whenever the characteristic mass advances by a
             fixed amount in log m0 gives approximately the same number
             of snapshots per mass decade.  This follows the support of
             the integrand and is the placement to use for closed runs
             spanning more than about two decades.

Whatever placement is chosen, the Riemann sum must use the actual
physical Delta t between neighbouring snapshots.

Population resampling [9] and snapshot placement [10] address different
problems.  Population control determines how much dynamic range can be
simulated; snapshot placement determines whether the observable is
measured correctly over that range.  A run can have exact dynamics and a
meaningless spectrum.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[11] FRAGMENT SPLIT WIDTH
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A fragmentation event divides a parent of mass m into xi*m and (1-xi)*m,
where

            xi ~ Uniform(0.5 - w, 0.5 + w),   w = frag_split_width.

The choice w = 0.5 corresponds to a uniform split fraction over (0,1).

Reducing w makes individual fragmentation steps more nearly equal and
therefore makes age and generation tracks narrower.  It also reduces the
dispersion in logarithmic mass added by each generation.

Successive generations overlap only after their random spread has become
comparable to their mean separation.  Schematically this requires

                    g ~ (mu / 2 sigma)^2

steps, where mu and sigma describe the mean and dispersion of the
logarithmic mass decrement, while the cascade takes about 6/mu of them.
If the split distribution is too narrow, the generations remain separated
through a significant part of the cascade and the spectrum develops a
persistent comb ("picket fence") at the characteristic logarithmic split
interval.  Below about w = 0.25 the modulation becomes significant.

This is a SYSTEMATIC effect, not ordinary Monte Carlo variance.
Increasing the number of particles does not fill masses that the
fragmentation rule itself rarely produces.  A power-law fit through such
a comb returns a shifted spectral index with a small formal uncertainty.

Narrow split distributions should therefore be checked against the raw
mass histogram.  Narrowing the split is not the way to obtain narrow age
or generation sequences; that is what `frag_age_rule` does, note [7].


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[12] WARM START
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A cold open-system run starts with its population concentrated near the
injection scale and must build the stationary cascade across the entire
mass interval before the steady spectrum can be measured.

The relevant cost is of order the mass residence time,

                    tau_res = M_sys / (q m_inj),

i.e. the time required for the injected flux to replace the mass stored
in the box.  Across a broad inertial range this transient can dominate
the computational cost.

A warm start instead initialises the population with a power-law
distribution spanning the cascade, ic={'steady': {...}}.

This can remove most of the filling transient, but it is legitimate only
if the stationary spectrum is demonstrated to be an ATTRACTOR rather than
imposed by the initial condition.  The validation is to initialise the
system with deliberately incorrect slopes, preferably on both sides of
the predicted stationary value, and verify that the independent runs
converge to the same final spectrum.  Starting from the theoretical slope
and recovering that same slope is not an independent demonstration of
convergence.

A synthetic warm population has a second limitation: seeded particles
have no physical ancestry inside the simulation, so their split count,
injection age and family history are undefined.  Consequently:

  - the steady-state mass spectrum may be measured from a validated warm
    start;
  - ancestry-based observables such as generation, inherited age and
    family history must exclude synthetic seed descendants until those
    have been replaced by particles originating from the real injection
    channel.

The code marks this distinction explicitly, note [13], so that
acceleration of the stationary-spectrum calculation cannot contaminate
the trajectory and generation diagnostics.

The seeded masses are drawn by inverse CDF in logarithmic variables
(`sample_powerlaw`), because the direct power form loses most of the
mantissa at the light end over several decades.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[13] THE INHERITED HONESTY MARK: gen = -1
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Seeded bodies receive gen = -1 instead of 0, and the mark propagates:

    fragmentation: gen(child)  = -1 if gen(parent) < 0, else gen+1
    coagulation:   gen(merged) = -1 if either parent is < 0
    injection:     gen = 0

so contamination can spread but never heal.

Everything with gen < 0 is excluded from gen_counts, gen_num, gen_tau and
the overflow count.  `honest_num` and `honest_mass` report, at every
snapshot, the fraction of the population that is free of seed ancestry.
Seeded bodies wash out on the residence time, so the honest fraction is a
direct readout of how far a run has progressed.

`gen_time` of a seeded body is -inf rather than 0, so that a missing age
is obviously absent rather than silently plausible.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[14] THE WAITING LAW
----------------------------------------------------------------------

For a particle sitting at mass m, let dt be the time until the next event
that CHANGES ITS MASS (it is disrupted, or it merges).  Its mass is
constant over that whole interval by construction, so the interval
belongs unambiguously to one mass bin, and

    <dt>(m) ~ 1/nu(m),    nu(m) = Int K(m,m') n(m') dm'.

Since one event changes m by a factor of order unity, dm ~ m, and with
the growth law m ~ t^b, dm/dt ~ m^(1-1/b):

                    <dt> ~ dm / (dm/dt) ~ m^(1/b).

The observable therefore measures b directly, without going through
alpha, where db/dalpha = b^2 amplifies the error.

Three properties make it usable on a warm start:

  - MEMORYLESS.  It asks nothing about where the particle came from, so
    seeded bodies contribute from the first event.
  - LOCAL.  The interval is attributed to the bin the particle actually
    sits in, not to a trajectory crossing the whole range, so the sink
    censors only the bins next to it.
  - UNBIASED BY CONSTRUCTION.  Every interval is recorded exactly once,
    at the moment it ends, so there is no length bias from inspecting the
    population at snapshot times.  The only loss is censoring of the
    intervals still open when the run stops.

The price is dynamic range: 1/b is small, so <dt> changes slowly across
the range.  A shallow slope needs a long arm and clean bins, so the fit
should use a guard band excluding a decade at each end.

Both an UNGATED accumulator (from t = 0) and a GATED one (after the
iso_start_sink gate) are kept, so the size of the transient contamination
is measurable rather than assumed.

THE DISTRIBUTED SINK CENSORS, IT DOES NOT COUNT.  A particle removed by
the sink of note [16] did not change mass; its interval was cut short.
`wait_close` is therefore not called on it, and two estimators are kept
side by side:

  - `exposure` = Int N_b dt / (number of stays that ENDED).  The
    occupancy the removed particle accrued is already in the numerator,
    because delete_particle flushes the integral before N_bin moves,
    while its unfinished stay never enters the denominator.  Both halves
    are consistent, so the estimator stays unbiased at any xi0.
  - `interval` = mean over stays that ended.  Censored stays are missing,
    and they are the long ones, so the mean is pulled down where the sink
    acts most strongly.

The gap between the two is a consistency check when the sink is off and a
measure of its strength when it is on.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[15] RESTART FROM A SAVED STATE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

ic={'state': {...}} restores mass, gen, gen_time and inj_time from a
finished run.  Nothing is reset.  After a cold run every particle already
carries an honest split count -- the number of breakups since its
ancestor entered at m_inj -- so the relation between generation and mass
is intact and the honest fraction of note [13] is 1 from the first event.
This is the difference from the synthetic power-law seed of note [12],
where there is no history to inherit.

THE TIME OFFSET.  Every inherited timestamp is shifted back by the
previous leg's end time and therefore lands negative.  Those particles
did enter before t = 0, so tau = t_phys - t_anc returns the full age
across the seam instead of restarting it.  Without this, a run shorter
than one residence time yields a tau(g) curve that saturates at t_end and
an apparent growth exponent that is censored downwards.

RESTARTS COMPOSE.  Each leg subtracts its own end time and adds it to
`t_origin`, so t_origin + final_t_phys is the total age of the box over
any number of legs.  The run length therefore does not have to be guessed
in advance: run, checkpoint, test stationarity, and either stop or
continue from the checkpoint.

HOW LONG IS ENOUGH.  Two commonly used criteria do not detect a spectrum
that is still evolving.  The ratio of removed to injected mass satisfies

    M_out = M_in + M_sys(0) - M_sys(t),   so   M_out/M_in = 1 + dM/M_in,

which tends to 1 because M_in grows without bound; it says only that the
run outlived the filling transient.  The same applies to the number of
absorbed particles per event.  Stationarity should instead be judged on
<m> and alpha compared between the two halves of the tail of the run.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[16] THE DISTRIBUTED SINK
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A removal acting on every particle at once,

    xi(m) = xi0 * (m/m_ref)^omega          [rate PER PARTICLE],

as opposed to `sink_mass`, which is an absorbing wall at one end of the
range.  Being first order in the population, it enters the stationary
balance as a loss term linear in n,

                    d(rho v_m)/dm = -xi(m) rho.

The absorbing wall is the omega -> infinity limit of the same object;
both channels are available at once and do not interfere.

THE ALGORITHM, in three parts.

 (i) THE RATE, from the population and nothing else.  The engine keeps

                S_pow = sum over live particles of m^omega

     exactly, by O(1) updates at every mass change: injection,
     coagulation, fragmentation, absorption at the wall, and the sink
     itself.  The total removal rate is xi0 * w * S_pow.  Once per
     snapshot S_pow is re-derived from the array and the worst relative
     discrepancy is reported as out["siphon_spow_drift"].  This is a bug
     detector, not a tolerance: a few 1e-9 is float64 cancellation, orders
     of magnitude more is a missed update.

 (ii) THE QUOTA, deterministic, note [6].  siphon_residual accumulates
     xi_amp * S_pow * dt and only its integer part fires; the remainder is
     carried forward.  No Poisson number is drawn, so the channel adds no
     shot noise.  The weight cancels exactly, note [5ii].

 (iii) THE VICTIM, by rejection at the level of the POPULATION.  Draw a
     particle uniformly and accept with probability (m/m_bound)^omega.
     The normalisation cancels:

        P(i) = [xi0 S_pow dt] * (m_i/m_b)^omega / sum_j (m_j/m_b)^omega
             = xi0 m_i^omega dt,

     so the scheme samples xi(m_i) dt independently for every particle,
     exactly, and needs no histogram, no cumulative sum and no tree.

WHY NOT "DRAW A MASS FROM m^omega AND TAKE THE NEAREST PARTICLE".  The
spacing between neighbouring masses is the inverse of the local density,
so snapping a draw to the nearest body gives

                P(i) ~ m_i^omega dm_i ~ m_i^omega / n(m_i),

i.e. an effective rate xi_eff = xi0 m^omega / n(m), non-linear in n.  The
loss term is then not -xi rho, and such a sink removes a fixed number per
unit time from the sparse end of the range instead of a fixed fraction.

THE BOUND.  Part (iii) needs the supremum of m^omega over the LIVE
population.  In each of the four configurations it is known before the run
starts and never moves:

    fragmentation, omega < 0 : nothing alive is lighter than the sink
    fragmentation, omega > 0 : nothing ever gets heavier than what entered
    coagulation,   omega > 0 : nothing alive is heavier than the sink
    coagulation,   omega < 0 : nothing ever gets lighter than what entered

The extremum over {sink, injection mass, seed} covers all four and stays
valid when the seed extends past the wall.  A running record of m_min or
m_max would degrade instead: in fragmentation m_min is written on a
fragment before the wall deletes it, so the record walks below sink_mass
and the acceptance never recovers.  A violated bound raises, exactly as a
violated pair majorant does, note [2].

THE COST.  Acceptance is <(m/m_b)^omega> over the live spectrum.  For a
small |omega| on a power-law spectrum this is of order one, so the channel
costs little; it becomes expensive only at |omega| of order one, where a
per-bin bound would be needed instead.

EVENTS ARE SEPARATE, MASS IS SUMMED.  `sink_events`/`n_out` and
`siphon_events`/`n_siphon` are never mixed, and neither are `M_out` and
`M_siphon`: the two routes answer different questions.  Two places add
them, for two different reasons:

  - STATIONARITY, on mass.  M_removed = M_out + M_siphon, and the
    criterion is M_removed/M_in -> 1.  Sink events alone say nothing about
    settling, because a strong distributed sink removes material near the
    injection scale while nothing has crossed the range; mass is conserved
    whichever way it left.  `mass_drift` uses the same sum and stays
    identically zero.
  - THE GATES, on counts, when gate_counts_siphon is on (the default
    whenever the distributed sink is).  Only (m_sink/m_inj)^delta0 of the
    flux reaches the wall, so a gate counting the wall alone would open
    much later and would stop runs at different delta0 at different
    degrees of convergence.  Total removal equals total injection in the
    steady state at any delta0, which makes the sum the delta0-independent
    gate.  The price is that it no longer certifies that mass crossed the
    whole range, only that the box turns over.

CALIBRATION.  The analytic prediction is written in

                delta0 = xi0/D,     nu = omega + 1 - beta,

where D, the drift amplitude, is emergent rather than an input and must be
measured on a pilot run with siphon=None.  Two routes exist.

THE FLUX ROUTE.  In the steady state the mass flux in mass space is the
injected one, J = q m_inj, and the Eulerian drift velocity follows from it
and from the measured spectrum alone:

    v(m) = J/rho(m),  rho = m F(m)
      =>  tau_drift(m) = m/|v| = m^2 F(m)/J = (A_F/J) m^(2+alpha),

with A_F and alpha the amplitude and index of the fitted spectrum.  The
exponent obeys 2 + alpha = 1 - beta, so it doubles as a check on the fit.
Nothing here assumes anything about the collision integral, so it holds
for both processes.  It is passed as
siphon={'delta0':.., 'nu':.., 'tau_drift':(A_F/J, 2+alpha)}.

THE WAITING-LAW ROUTE.  <dt> = A m^(1/b) with 1/b = 1 - beta gives
tau_drift = <dt>/c, c = out["dlnm_mean"].  It is the sharper measurement
where the collision integral is LOCAL.  Fragmentation with a disruption
threshold f > 0 is local, note [8], and there the two routes agree.
Coagulation off a spectrum steeper than m^-1 is not: nu(m) is dominated by
m' ~ m_inj, so <dt> falls with mass and even the sign of the exponent is
not 1 - beta.  Both numbers are finite, so the engine cannot detect this;
the choice of route belongs to the caller.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[17] THE LOCALITY WINDOW: IMPLEMENTATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Two bodies interact only if their masses are comparable,

                    m_small >= f * m_large,

set by `frag_min_ratio` for fragmentation and `coag_min_ratio` for
coagulation.  f = 0 removes the condition.  The physical motivation is
note [8]; this note describes the implementation and what it does and does
not change.

The same rule applies to both processes.  Without it, fragmentation
connects the sink scale directly to every mass above it, and coagulation
has the mirror-image problem: with a kernel that does not vanish for
m' << m, a large body grows mostly by absorbing the smallest ones, so
every scale is tied to the injection scale instead of to its neighbours.

WHEN IT MATTERS.  The constant-flux solution n ~ m^-p exists only where
the collision integral carrying the flux converges.  At the edge of the
convergence window the integral diverges logarithmically from the m' << m
region, the cascade is non-local, and the spectrum is set by the
boundaries rather than by the kernel.  The additive kernel, lambda = 1,
sits exactly there.  The signature is visible in the data:

  - the local slope of a lossless run does not collapse as a function of
    m, but does collapse as a function of log m / log m_sink;
  - Gamma crosses -(1+beta) near the middle of the range whatever the box
    size;
  - the total swing of Gamma across the range is the same in boxes of
    different size, so a larger box stretches the same curve and never
    produces a plateau.

Below the edge of the window, lambda < 1, the integral converges by itself
and f only trims a region that contributes little.

WHAT IT DOES NOT CHANGE.  The condition involves the ratio alone, so

                K_eff = K * Theta[min/max - f]

is homogeneous of the same degree as K.  beta = (1+lambda)/2, b and alpha
are unchanged.  Mass conservation is untouched: a forbidden pair is a NULL
EVENT, and null events change nothing.

WHAT IT DOES CHANGE.  The flux amplitude, downwards, because the
interactions removed are the ones that carried most of it; the same
spectrum is therefore reached more slowly in physical time.  The random
stream also differs from an unrestricted run, so runs at different f are
statistically comparable but not bit-comparable.

HOW IT IS IMPLEMENTED, in two places that are one rule.

 (a) THE BAND, in the majorant table.  A bin pair whose most favourable
     member already fails the test has K_eff = 0 throughout, and its entry
     is zeroed.  Without this, the test would be enforced by rejection
     alone and the acceptance would collapse: at f = 0.3 on a 0.1 dex grid
     only about a dozen bins around the diagonal survive.

     This is exact, not an approximation.  A trial selects a pair with
     probability fmaj_ij/R and accepts with K_ij/fmaj_ij, so the realised
     rate is proportional to K_ij and fmaj cancels.  Deleting pairs with
     K_eff = 0 lowers R, which lengthens dt = 2V/(wR) per trial by exactly
     the factor by which the number of trials falls.  The physical clock
     does not move.

 (b) THE TEST, per pair, before the majorant thinning.  Bins have width,
     so pairs at the edge of the band are only partly allowed and are
     checked individually.  The test is placed before the thinning draw
     because a banded-out pair has fmaj = 0 while K > 0 and would
     otherwise trip the majorant-violation guard.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[18] THE MASS GRID
----------------------------------------------------------------------

`edges` is a 1D strictly increasing array of mass-bin edges, supplied by
the caller.  It fixes three things at once:

  - the bins used for O(1) membership, note [3];
  - the bin pairs of the majorant table, note [2], whose entries are built
    from the upper corners of each bin, so the corner overshoot -- and
    hence the acceptance -- is set by the bin width;
  - the resolution of every histogram the engine returns.

The grid is logarithmic in practice.  Counts are accumulated per bin and
converted to dN/dm by dividing by the bin width, so the returned `dndm` is
a density in m, not in ln m.  A slope quoted as dN/dm ~ m^alpha is
therefore related to a log-binned histogram by
dn/dln m ~ m^(alpha+1).

The grid must extend beyond the working range at both ends.  A rising last
bin indicates pile-up: the flux has nowhere to go, and both alpha and b
are affected.  Keep the characteristic mass at least two decades inside
max(edges), or use an absorbing boundary and drop the extreme bins from
every fit.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[19] RANDOM NUMBERS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

All randomness is drawn from a single `numpy.random.Generator` passed as
`rng`.  If none is given, a fresh default_rng is created, and the run is
then not reproducible.

The engine does not record the generator's seed.  The metadata field
`seed` describes the warm-start power-law seed of note [12] -- its index,
range and particle count -- not the random number generator.
Reproducibility of a run is therefore the caller's responsibility:
construct the Generator with an explicit seed and store that seed
alongside the output.

Channels that are switched off draw no random numbers at all, so enabling
or disabling them changes the stream.  Two runs are comparable
bit-for-bit only at identical settings; otherwise they are comparable
statistically.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[20] WHAT A RUN RETURNS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

`simulate` returns a dict of arrays, one entry per recorded snapshot,
together with a `meta` sub-dict and the final per-particle state.

The arrays include the spectrum `dndm` on the grid of note [18], the
characteristic mass and mean mass, the isochrone histogram in (age, mass)
and the generation histogram in (gen, mass), note [7], the waiting-law
accumulators of note [14], the counters of both removal channels, and the
bookkeeping residuals `mass_drift`, `siphon_spow_drift`, `gen_overflow`
and `honest_num`/`honest_mass`.

`meta` records the configuration in full: process, system, kernel name and
its homogeneity degree lambda, the initial condition, the boundary
settings, the locality ratio actually in force and the number of banded
bins, the split width and both age rules, gen_max, the particle weight and
volume, the clock and injection schemes as text, the gate settings, and
the stop reason.

The final state -- masses, generations, generation times and injection
times -- is what `state_of` extracts and `ic={'state': ...}` consumes,
note [15].  `save_run` and `load_run` write and read the whole dict.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
[21] BUILT-IN CHECKS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The engine raises rather than continues when an assumption it relies on
is violated.  The checks, and what each one catches:

  K_ij <= fmaj_ij, every trial.  Catches an invalid majorant, note [2],
  which would otherwise bias the acceptance silently.

  m <= m_bound for the distributed sink, every draw.  The same check for
  the removal channel, note [16].

  `mass_drift`, the residual of M_sys = M_ref + M_in - (M_out + M_siphon).
  Identically zero at w = 1, note [9], so any nonzero value is a
  bookkeeping bug.

  `siphon_spow_drift`, the relative discrepancy between the incrementally
  maintained S_pow and its direct recomputation, note [16i].

  `gen_overflow`, the number of particles above gen_max, note [7].  A
  nonzero count means the tail of the generation distribution is folded
  into the last bin and the linearity test is invalid there.

  `max_stall_tries`, the number of majorant attempts without an accepted
  event.  Catches a collapsed acceptance, which usually means a badly
  chosen grid or a locality band that has removed nearly everything,
  notes [2] and [17].

  Argument validation at entry: process, system, both age rules, gen_max,
  the split half-width, and the combinations that do not exist -- a sink
  gate in a closed system, an open system without injection or without an
  absorbing boundary.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
FAILURE MAP: WHICH SYMPTOM POINTS AT WHICH NOTE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The effects above produce overlapping fingerprints.

  alpha -> -2 for every kernel
        clock advanced per event instead of per trial ............ [5iv]
        non-local interaction rule, f = 0 .......................... [8]
        (the two are indistinguishable from the spectrum alone; check
         the acceptance and the value of the locality ratio)

  alpha shifted, spectrum visibly combed
        split half-width below about 0.25 ......................... [11]

  alpha clean, plateau finder reports a tight one-decade plateau on a
  CLOSED run that is nonetheless wrong
        snapshot placement 'events' ............................... [10]

  an age class as wide as the whole inertial range, whatever the age
  rule is set to (median age equal to maximum age under 'inherit')
        expected: an age class is an ensemble, not a path ........... [7]

  a generation histogram with everything in gen = 0
        the population has not been ground yet, or the run is shorter
        than one waiting time at m_inj; with a locality ratio > 0 a body
        at the top has few partners of comparable mass ....... [7], [17]

  <x> or Var(x) not linear in gen, through the origin
        the splits are correlated, or gen_max is clipping and the tail
        is folded into the last bin; check gen_overflow ............. [7]

  alpha clean but b too low
        the growth-law fit window reaches into the shelf near m_inj,
        where <m>(tau) has not left the injection mass.  Fit the plateau
        in dlog<m>/dlog tau rather than reusing the spectrum's guard
        band ...................................................... [14]

  alpha too shallow AND b too low AND the last mass bin rising
        pile-up at the top of the grid ............................ [18]

  the distributed sink is on and alpha moved, but not by delta0
        check nu = omega + 1 - beta.  Only nu = 0 displaces the index;
        at s*nu > 0 the spectrum keeps its lossless slope and grows a
        stretched-exponential cutoff, and a fit reaching into that
        cutoff reports a spurious shift ........................... [16]

  the distributed sink is on and the run crawls, channel acceptance far
  below one
        the rejection bound is far outside the live population; read
        out["siphon_acceptance"] and out["siphon_m_bound"] ......... [16]

  M_out/M_in flattens well below 1 while mass_drift is still zero
        nothing is lost; the sink mass is missing from the criterion.
        Use M_removed = M_out + M_siphon .......................... [16]

  the run never stops, or the isochrones never start accumulating
        gate_counts_siphon is off with a strong distributed sink ... [16]

  out["siphon_spow_drift"] far above 1e-8
        a mass change somewhere does not update S_pow.  This is a bug,
        not a statistical excursion ............................... [16]


----------------------------------------------------------------------
ANALYTIC PREDICTIONS THE ENGINE IS BUILT TO MEASURE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

With kernel homogeneity K(a m1, a m2) = a^lambda K(m1,m2), and

    dm0/dtau  ~  m0^beta                        (drift)
    m0        ~  tau^b,   b = 1/(1-beta)        (growth)
    F(m)      ~  m^alpha, alpha = -(1+beta)     (spectrum)

  CLOSED (packet amplitude ~ m0^-2, mass conserving):
      beta = lambda,         b = 1/(1-lambda),  alpha = -(1+lambda)

  OPEN (background amplitude ~ m0^alpha, self-consistent):
      beta = (1+lambda)/2,   b = 2/(1-lambda),  alpha = -(3+lambda)/2

Fragmentation gives the same beta and the same alpha; only the sign of
the drift flips, so m0 ~ (tau_* - tau)^b instead of tau^b.

Both branches meet at lambda = 1 (beta = 1), where the drift becomes
exponential and alpha = -2.  That point is simultaneously the gelation
threshold and the only case in which closed and open agree.

    constant kernel,  lambda = 0  : closed alpha = -1,    b = 1
                                    open   alpha = -3/2,  b = 2
    geometric kernel, lambda = 2/3: closed alpha = -5/3,  b = 3
                                    open   alpha = -11/6, b = 6

PRECISION OF b.  Writing n(m) ~ m^-tau with tau = -alpha, the mass
integral in an open run is set by the upper cut-off, so b = 1/(2-tau) and
db/dtau = 1/(2-tau)^2.  For the geometric kernel 2 - tau = 1/6, hence
db/dtau = 36: a one per cent error in alpha moves b by a full unit.
Quote b to at most two significant figures, and check alpha to three
decimals before reading a discrepancy in b as physics.

SCOPE.  These predictions describe the INERTIAL RANGE only -- not the
neighbourhoods of the injection scale or the sink -- and assume pure
coagulation or pure fragmentation, never a mixture.  For fragmentation
they require a disruption threshold f > 0, note [8]; alpha is then
independent of f.

WITH A DISTRIBUTED SINK, note [16].  Write the drift as v_m = s D m^beta,
with s = +1 for coagulation and s = -1 for fragmentation, and the removal
as xi(m) = xi0 m^omega.  The family is governed by one exponent and one
number,

    nu = omega + 1 - beta,        delta0 = xi0/D,

with delta(m) = xi(m) tau_drift(m) = delta0 m^nu the fraction of a cohort
lost while the drift carries its scale up by a factor e.  A spectrum with
no intrinsic scale requires that fraction to have no scale either, i.e.
nu = 0.  Three regimes, distinguished by sign(s*nu):

  nu = 0, i.e. omega = beta - 1.  The removal is scale-matched to the
  drift, the cohort weight is a pure power, and the index is displaced by
  exactly delta0:

      p = 1 + beta + s*delta0,
          coagulation   p = 1 + beta + delta0   (steeper)
          fragmentation p = 1 + beta - delta0   (flatter)

  with dN/dm ~ m^-p, i.e. alpha = -p.  alpha then moves linearly in
  delta0, with slope one, and without the b^2 amplification.

  s*nu > 0.  The loss outruns the drift.  The index does not move; the
  spectrum keeps m^-(1+beta) and acquires a cutoff,

      dN/dm ~ m^-(1+beta) exp[-(s delta0/nu) m^nu].

  Below the cutoff the spectrum is exactly the lossless one.

  s*nu < 0.  The drift outruns the loss.  A finite fraction of each
  cohort is removed within a bounded interval of ln m near injection, the
  removal is absorbed into the normalisation, and the index is again the
  lossless p = 1 + beta.

nu decides WHETHER the index moves, s decides WHICH WAY.  The exponential
drift is the point omega = 0, beta = 1 of this family, where the sink may
be mass-independent because nu = 0 is then satisfied by omega = 0.

For the geometric kernel, open: beta = 5/6, so the marginal line is
omega = -1/6, and alpha reads -11/6 + delta0 in fragmentation.

WHAT DEPENDS ON WHAT.  alpha is a property of the steady-state spectrum
and is untouched by age bookkeeping; `age_rule` and `frag_age_rule`
cannot move it.  b is a property of a trajectory and depends on the age
rule.  The two are linked only through the closure above, so a
disagreement between them is information about the closure or about the
fit window, and not automatically an error in either.
